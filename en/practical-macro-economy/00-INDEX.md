# Practical macroeconomics for investors

**What moves markets, how the pieces connect, and how to read every chart in a macro-and-crypto video.**

> Polish version: [`pl/praktyczna-makroekonomia/`](../../pl/praktyczna-makroekonomia/00-INDEKS.md)

Macro commentary on YouTube moves fast: the Fed, the yield curve, the Sahm rule, net liquidity, bitcoin dominance,
the bull market support band, a "midterm year", all in one video. This course slows it down. Each indicator gets a
definition, where to get the data for free, how to read it, how it connects to the other indicators, and what it
has historically meant for bitcoin, altcoins, stocks, bonds, the dollar and gold.

The course uses the work of [Benjamin Cowen](https://www.youtube.com/@benjaminjcowen) (Into The Cryptoverse) as its
map of what to cover. He is one of the most-watched analysts who links macroeconomics to crypto markets, and he uses
a wide, consistent set of indicators. The aim is that after this course you understand every indicator, metric and
chart he shows, *and* can judge them yourself. Every indicator also stands on its own, so the course is useful even
if you never watch his videos.

**This course is independent.** It is not affiliated with, reviewed or endorsed by Benjamin Cowen or Into The
Cryptoverse. His views are summarised in our own words with dates and links; his proprietary metrics are explained
only conceptually.

## What makes it different

- **Connections, not just definitions.** Every indicator card says what leads it, what it leads, with what lag and
  why, with dated historical episodes and the cases where the pattern failed. Chapter 13 puts it all on one map.
- **Our own numbers.** A small, free [macro lab](../../tools/macro-lab/README.md) recomputes the key statistics from
  FRED data: yield-curve inversions vs recessions, Sahm-rule triggers, what followed each first Fed cut, the
  presidential cycle, bitcoin's moving averages and its correlation with stocks, the dollar and liquidity. You can
  rerun it any day. The course quotes the [30 October 2026 snapshot](../../tools/macro-lab/REPORT-2026-10-30.md).
- **Honest about limits.** Eight recessions and three bitcoin cycles are small samples. 2024–2026 broke several
  textbook sequences, and the course shows where.
- **Traceable.** Every chapter cites its sources inline. "Research notes A–E" are the published
  [working notes](../../research-notes/README.md) behind the course, with a [fact-check log](../../research-notes/FACT-CHECK-LOG.md).
- **Current to 30 October 2026**, including the 2025 bitcoin top, the 2026 oil shock and the Fed's September 2026 hike.

## Who it is for

Investors and serious learners who want to understand macro-driven markets: no economics degree needed, comfort with
percentages and charts assumed. Chapter 01 teaches the chart-reading toolkit (log scales, moving averages, regression
bands, correlation and lead/lag) that everything else relies on.

## Three ways to read it

1. **Complete (recommended, roughly 25–35 hours):** 01 → 16 in order. Each chapter ends with "Check yourself"
   questions.
2. **"I watch the videos and want to follow them" (about 8 hours):** 01 → 14 (decoder) → 13 → then the chapters the
   decoder sends you to.
3. **Macro only, no crypto (about 15 hours):** 01–09 → 13 → 15.

## How each indicator is presented

Every indicator in chapters 02–12 follows the same card:

- **What it is**: definition, publisher, frequency, release timing, revisions
- **Where to find it**: exact FRED id, TradingView ticker or website
- **How to read it**: levels vs change, thresholds that matter, common misreadings
- **How Cowen uses it**: the claim he makes with it, dated and linked
- **Connections**: what leads it and what it leads, the sign, typical lag, mechanism, episodes and exceptions
- **What it has meant for assets**: bitcoin, altcoins, stocks, bonds, dollar, gold
- **Investor takeaway**

## Important

This is educational material. It is **not financial advice**, not a trading system and not a forecast. Historical
relationships between indicators can and do break, and samples of a few cycles cannot prove a rule. Figures are as
of 30 October 2026 unless dated otherwise. Make your own decisions, and check data at the source before relying on it.
